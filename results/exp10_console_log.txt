[1b] Evaluating h without cancellation
  The textbook form h = 2(1 - sqrt(pq) - sqrt((1-p)(1-q))) subtracts two numbers of order
  one to make a result of order eps^2. This library uses the exact identity
    h = (p-q)^2 [ (sqrt(p)+sqrt(q))^-2 + (sqrt(1-p)+sqrt(1-q))^-2 ],
  which has no cancellation. Both are compared with the series of Proposition 2.1, which
  is independent of either.
         eps       stable     textbook         gain
       1e-03     1.18e-05     1.18e-05           1x
       1e-04     1.19e-07     1.22e-07        1.03x
       1e-05     1.19e-09     5.77e-07         485x
       1e-06     1.39e-11     4.95e-05    3.57e+06x
  the stable form keeps falling as eps^2, the quartic residual; the textbook form turns round and grows once cancellation dominates
  Acceptance: the two agree at eps = 1e-3, and at eps = 1e-6 the stable form is 3.6e+06x more accurate  PASS

[2b] The constants A ln 2 and A zeta(2a) hold as c -> 0, not at the physical c
  A is the coefficient of the quadratic term only. By Proposition 2.1 the next term is
  cubic, so the exact sum falls below the quadratic prediction and the relative deviation
  is O(c), not O(c^2). At p_0 = 1/2 it would be O(c^2), which is why Paper I could use
  c = 0.1 and still match its constants to ten digits.
